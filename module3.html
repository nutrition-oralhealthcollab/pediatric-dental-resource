Diet, Nutrition, and Oral Health

Exploring the intersection of dietetic interventions and pediatric dental outcomes. A clinical guide for dietitians in multidisciplinary care.

verified Key Reference

Based on AAPD Dietary Policies for Infants, Children, and Adolescents.

warning

monitoring Cariogenic Properties of Supplements

Dietary interventions for complex pediatric needs often introduce increased dental risks. Recognizing the cariogenic potential of nutritional supports is essential for integrated preventive care.

liquids Thickened Liquids

Starch-based agents increase oral clearance time, prolonging exposure to fermentable carbohydrates.

Clinical Insight: Reduced salivary flow increases demineralization risk.

nutrition High-Calorie Formulas

Oral supplements frequently have high sugar content to meet intensive caloric needs.

  • High viscosity increases stickiness
  • Intake frequency is often more damaging than total volume

Managing 'Grazing'

Continuous exposure to food prevents oral pH from returning to a neutral state, maintaining a constant acid attack on enamel.

Oral PH Neutralization Phase Progress

  • Acid Attack Triggered immediately upon carbohydrate ingestion.
  • Neutralization Typically takes 20-30 minutes post-intake.

Integrated Care Plans

Dietitians are uniquely positioned to integrate dental risk screening into standard nutritional assessments.
