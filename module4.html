Module 4: Feeding Behaviors, Oral Habits & Dental Consequences

Learning Objectives:

  1. 1. Connect specific oral habits (non-nutritive sucking, digit sucking, prolonged pacifier use, and tongue thrusting) to their expected dental sequelae, including malocclusion type and severity.
  2. 2. Identify bruxism in pediatric patients, describe its etiological associations in feeding team populations, and explain the dental management and feeding implications of tooth wear.
  3. 3. Recognize how oral sensory processing differences (hyposensitivity and hypersensitivity) affect oral hygiene compliance, food acceptance, and collaboration with dental providers.
  4. 4. Understand the oral health implications of drooling and oral motor dysfunction in neurologically impaired children.

1. Non-Nutritive Oral Habits

Non-nutritive sucking (NNS) is common in early development; however, prolonged persistence beyond age 3 significantly increases the risk of dental malocclusion.

Pacifier Use

Often results in a wider anterior open bite and narrower maxillary arch compared to digit habits, as the nipple shape influences tongue positioning.

Digit Sucking

Typically leads to increased overjet, proclination of maxillary incisors, and localized alveolar changes based on the finger's pressure vector.

2. Bruxism in Complex Pediatrics

Bruxism in children with neurological or developmental conditions is often multifactorial, involving gastroesophageal reflux, sleep apnea, or dopamine-pathway dysfunction.

Type Characteristics Clinical Concern
Sleep-Related Rhythmic masticatory muscle activity; grinding sounds. Sleep disordered breathing (SDB) screening required.
Awake Bruxism Often associated with stress or high-intensity focus. TMJ tension and enamel wear in primary dentition.

3. Sensory Aversions

Oral hypersensitivity often leads to significant oral care challenges. Children may exhibit gagging, distress, or physical avoidance during toothbrushing, which compromises periodontal health.

Desensitization Strategies
Systematic Desensitization

Gradual tactile input from extremities toward the perioral and intraoral regions.

Vibration

Use of Z-Vibe or electric brushes to modulate sensory threshold.

Environmental Control

Reducing auditory/visual triggers in the dental operatory.

4. Oral Motor Dysfunction

Inefficient bolus clearance and poor salivary management create a high-caries-risk environment, particularly in children with cerebral palsy or muscular dystrophies.

80%

Higher risk of skin breakdown (Perioral)

3x

Increased likelihood of bolus stasis

Caution: Salivary stasis combined with frequent carbohydrate exposure in thickeners significantly accelerates enamel demineralization.
