Feeding Behaviors, Oral Habits & Dental Consequences

Comprehensive SLP clinical focus on the intersection of myofunctional development and pediatric dental health.

child_care

psychology Non-Nutritive Sucking (NNS)

Evaluation of intensity, duration, and frequency is critical for speech and dental health. Digit habits are often more recalcitrant and cause greater dental arch deformation.

Digit vs. Pacifier Use

Evaluation of habitual intensity. Persistent use leads to anterior open bite and increased overjet.

Dental Consequences

Posterior crossbite due to buccal musculature pressure during prolonged NNS suction.

Sensory Oral Aversion

Barriers to feeding success often manifest as defensive behaviors toward specific textures.

  • warning Hyper-responsivity
  • texture Texture Selectivity

SLP Intervention Tip

Transition from "extinction" models to "systematic desensitization" for long-term oral motor success.

Tongue Thrust & OMD

Tongue thrust is a physiological imbalance. When the tongue pushes forward against teeth, it can exert up to 500 grams of pressure, causing significant dental displacement over time.

80% OMD Co-morbidity
4.2x Relapse Risk
